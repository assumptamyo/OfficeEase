Lesson 1.1

A presentation is a sequence of slides that supports a speaker; it is not a document to be read aloud. Before designing anything, you need to know where PowerPoint keeps its tools and how to build and arrange slides. This lesson introduces the workspace.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • identify the panes of the PowerPoint window and the purpose of each;
  • add, duplicate, reorder and delete slides;
  • organise a long presentation into sections and start a slide show.

The workspace

In Normal view the window is divided into three working areas beneath the Ribbon.

Slide thumbnail pane
The column on the left showing a small image of every slide in order. It is where you select, reorder, duplicate and delete slides.
Slide pane
The large central area showing the current slide. All editing of text, pictures and shapes happens here.
Notes pane
The strip beneath the slide for speaker notes: reminders for the presenter that the audience never sees. Show or hide it with the Notes button on the status bar.
Status bar
The bottom edge of the window, showing the slide number and holding the view buttons and the zoom slider.

The Ribbon tabs

The tabs follow the order in which a presentation is normally built.

TabUsed for

Working with slides

TaskHow
Add a slideHome > New Slide or Ctrl + M
Duplicate a slideSelect its thumbnail and press Ctrl + D
ReorderDrag the thumbnail up or down
DeleteSelect the thumbnail and press Delete
Select severalHold Ctrl (separate slides) or Shift (a run of slides) while clicking
TipDuplicating a slide you have already designed is faster than starting a new one, and it keeps positions and formatting consistent from slide to slide.

Sections

A long presentation is easier to manage when its slides are grouped into sections, much like chapters. Right-click between two thumbnails and choose Add Section, then name it, for example Introduction, Results, Next Steps. A section can be collapsed, moved as a block, or handed to a colleague to complete.

Running the show

KeyAction
F5Start the slide show from the first slide
Shift + F5Start from the current slide
→, Space or clickNext slide
←Previous slide
EscEnd the show
Common mistakeEditing in the Slide pane and never running the show until the day of the presentation. Press Shift + F5 often; a slide looks different at full screen.

Worked example: the skeleton of a talk

  1. Open a blank presentation and type the title of your talk on Slide 1.
  2. Press Ctrl + M four times and type one heading on each new slide.
  3. Drag the last slide to second position in the thumbnail pane.
  4. Click in the Notes pane of Slide 1 and type what you will say to open.
  5. Press F5 and step through the slides with the arrow key.

Key takeaways

  • Normal view has three areas: thumbnails, the slide and the notes.
  • Ctrl + M adds a slide; Ctrl + D duplicates one.
  • Speaker notes are for the presenter and are hidden from the audience.
  • F5 starts the show from the beginning; Shift + F5 from the current slide.

Check your understanding

Where do you type reminders that only the presenter should see?

In the Notes pane beneath the slide.

Which pane do you use to change the order of slides in Normal view?

The slide thumbnail pane on the left: drag a thumbnail to its new position.

You are editing slide 14 and want to preview it at full screen. Which keys do you press?

Shift + F5, which starts the show from the current slide.
