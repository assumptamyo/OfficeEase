Lesson 2.2

Shapes are the building blocks of diagrams, cards, timelines and infographics. What separates a professional slide from an amateur one is rarely the shapes themselves but their alignment: equal sizes, level edges and even gaps. PowerPoint can guarantee all three.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • draw, duplicate and style shapes precisely;
  • align and distribute objects with the Arrange tools;
  • group objects and control their stacking order;
  • create custom shapes with Merge Shapes.

Drawing shapes precisely

Choose a shape from Insert > Shapes and drag on the slide. Modifier keys control the result.

HoldWhileEffect
ShiftdrawingForces a perfect square or circle, or a straight line at 45° steps
ShiftresizingKeeps the proportions of the shape
Ctrldrawing or resizingGrows the shape from its centre
CtrldraggingMakes a copy of the shape
Ctrl + ShiftdraggingMakes a copy that stays in line with the original

To add text, select the shape and type. Style it on the Shape Format tab with Shape Fill (the inside colour), Shape Outline (the border) and Shape Effects (shadow, glow, bevel).

TipUse effects sparingly. A soft shadow can lift a card off the background; several effects combined look dated. Flat colours with no outline are the safest modern choice.

Align and distribute

Select two or more objects, then choose Shape Format > Align (also on Home > Arrange).

Align
Lines objects up along a shared edge or centre: Left, Center, Right horizontally; Top, Middle, Bottom vertically.
Distribute
Makes the gaps between three or more objects equal: Distribute Horizontally or Distribute Vertically.

As you drag an object, red dashed Smart Guides appear when it is level with, or evenly spaced from, its neighbours. They are helpful for quick positioning, but the Align commands are exact.

Common mistakePositioning shapes by eye. Gaps that differ by even a few pixels are visible on a large screen and make a slide look careless. Always finish with Align and Distribute.

Grouping and stacking order

Grouping joins several objects so that they move, resize and copy as one. Select them and press Ctrl + G; press Ctrl + Shift + G to ungroup. You can still click an individual object inside a group to edit it.

Objects on a slide are stacked in layers. Change the order with Bring Forward, Bring to Front, Send Backward and Send to Back. The Selection Pane (Home > Arrange > Selection Pane) lists every object on the slide, lets you rename them, hide them and drag them into a new order; it is essential on a crowded slide.

Merge Shapes

When no built-in shape fits, combine several. Select two or more overlapping shapes and choose Shape Format > Merge Shapes.

OptionResult
UnionAll the shapes fused into one outline
CombineThe shapes joined, with the overlapping area cut out
FragmentEvery overlapping region becomes a separate piece
IntersectOnly the area where the shapes overlap is kept
SubtractThe later shapes are cut out of the first one selected

The order of selection matters: the result takes the colour of the shape selected first, and Subtract removes from that shape.

Worked example: three pricing cards

  1. Draw one rounded rectangle and give it a white fill, no outline and a subtle shadow.
  2. Hold Ctrl + Shift and drag it sideways twice to make three cards on one level.
  3. Select all three and choose Distribute Horizontally.
  4. Add a heading and price to each card as text boxes, then group each card with its text using Ctrl + G.
  5. Select the three groups and choose Align Middle to confirm they are level.

Key takeaways

  • Hold Shift while drawing to get a perfect square or circle.
  • Align lines up edges; Distribute equalises gaps.
  • Ctrl + G groups objects; Ctrl + Shift + G ungroups them.
  • Merge Shapes builds custom shapes from simple ones.

Check your understanding

Which key must be held while drawing to force a perfect square or circle?

Shift.

What is the shortcut for grouping several selected objects into one?

Ctrl + G.

Four icons are level but unevenly spaced. Which command fixes this?

Distribute Horizontally, which makes the gaps between them equal.
