Lesson 4.1

A transition is the visual effect shown as one slide gives way to the next. Used with discipline, transitions help an audience follow the structure of a talk. This lesson covers the standard transitions and Morph, which animates objects smoothly from one slide to the next.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • distinguish a transition from an animation;
  • apply a transition and set its options, duration and advance timing;
  • build a Morph transition between two slides;
  • judge when a transition helps and when it distracts.

A transition belongs to the slide it leads into. To animate the change from slide 2 to slide 3, apply the transition to slide 3.

Applying a transition

  1. Select the slide in the thumbnail pane.
  2. On the Transitions tab, choose an effect from the gallery, such as Fade, Push or Wipe.
  3. Open Effect Options to set its direction or variant.
  4. Set the Duration in seconds; a shorter time gives a quicker effect.
  5. Click Apply To All to use the same transition throughout the presentation.
TransitionAppearanceSuggests
FadeThe old slide dissolves into the new oneA calm continuation; suitable everywhere
PushThe new slide pushes the old one off the screenMoving on to the next step in a sequence
WipeThe new slide is revealed from one edgeUncovering new information

A star beside a thumbnail shows that the slide has a transition. Click Preview to watch it.

Advancing slides

The Advance Slide options on the Transitions tab decide what moves the show forward.

  • On Mouse Click is the default. The presenter controls the pace.
  • After a set time advances the slide automatically. This suits unattended displays such as an exhibition stand or a reception screen.

The Morph transition

Morph compares two consecutive slides and animates every object that appears on both: its position, size, rotation and colour change smoothly from the first slide to the second. The audience sees objects glide and grow rather than one slide replacing another.

  1. Design the first slide.
  2. Duplicate it (Ctrl + D). Duplicating is essential: Morph can only match objects that are the same object on both slides.
  3. On the duplicate, move, resize, rotate or recolour the objects into their final state.
  4. Select the second slide and choose Transitions > Morph.
  5. Press Shift + F5 on the first slide to watch the movement.

Under Effect Options, Morph can work on whole Objects, on Words or on individual Characters, which lets text rearrange itself.

NoteMorph is available in PowerPoint 2019 and Microsoft 365. In older versions the slides still appear, but with a simple fade. If an object is deleted and redrawn on the second slide instead of being carried over by duplication, it will fade rather than move.
TipMorph is ideal for zooming into part of a diagram, moving a highlight from one item to the next, or showing a product from a new angle, all without building any animations.

Using transitions well

  • Choose one transition for the whole presentation; change it only to mark a new section.
  • Keep durations short, typically under one second.
  • Avoid elaborate effects in professional settings. If the audience notices the transition, it has drawn attention away from the message.

Worked example: a product zoom

  1. On Slide 1 place a product photograph at small size on the left.
  2. Duplicate the slide. On Slide 2 enlarge the photograph and move it to the right.
  3. Apply Morph to Slide 2 with a duration of 1.5 seconds.
  4. Apply Fade to all the other slides with Apply To All, then re-apply Morph to Slide 2.

Key takeaways

  • Transitions act between slides; animations act on objects within a slide.
  • A transition is applied to the slide it leads into.
  • Morph animates the movement and scaling of objects between two duplicate slides automatically.
  • Use one restrained transition consistently.

Check your understanding

Which transition automatically animates object movement and size changes between two duplicate slides?

Morph.

You want an effect as slide 4 changes to slide 5. Which slide receives the transition?

Slide 5, because a transition belongs to the slide it leads into.

Why must the second slide be a duplicate of the first for Morph to work properly?

Morph matches objects that are the same on both slides. Duplicating carries the objects over, so PowerPoint can animate their change.
