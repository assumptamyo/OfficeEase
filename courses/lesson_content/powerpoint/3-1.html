Lesson 3.1

A well-chosen photograph communicates in a second what a paragraph cannot. A stretched, blurred or badly cropped one damages the credibility of the whole presentation. This lesson covers how to insert, crop, isolate and refine pictures properly.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • insert and resize pictures without distorting them;
  • crop to a region, to an aspect ratio and to a shape;
  • remove the background of a picture;
  • adjust, compress and describe pictures for a professional result.

Inserting and resizing

Choose Insert > Pictures and select This Device, Stock Images or Online Pictures. Selecting a picture opens the Picture Format tab.

Resize from a corner handle. Corner handles keep the width and height in proportion. The handles on the sides change one dimension only, which stretches the image.

Common mistakeStretching a picture to fill a space. Faces and logos that are slightly too wide or too tall are noticed immediately. If the shape of the picture does not suit the space, crop it; never stretch it.

Cropping

Cropping hides the parts of a picture you do not want. Choose Picture Format > Crop and drag the black crop handles inward; click outside the picture to finish.

Crop optionWhat it doesTypical use
CropTrims the edges freelyRemoving distracting surroundings
Aspect RatioCrops to fixed proportions such as 1:1, 4:3 or 16:9Making several photographs the same shape; filling a 16:9 slide
Crop to ShapeReshapes the picture's frame, or mask, to a chosen geometric shape such as a circleRound portraits of team members
Fill / FitFill covers the frame and trims the excess; Fit shows the whole picture inside the frameAdjusting a picture inside an existing frame

With Crop to Shape, the picture itself is unchanged; only the outline through which it is seen takes the new shape. For a true circle rather than an oval, first crop to an aspect ratio of 1:1, then crop to the Oval shape.

TipCropped areas are hidden, not deleted, so you can re-crop later. They are removed permanently only when you compress the pictures with Delete cropped areas of pictures ticked.

Removing the background

Remove Background isolates the subject of a photograph so that it can sit directly on the slide's own background.

  1. Select the picture and choose Picture Format > Remove Background. The area PowerPoint intends to remove turns magenta.
  2. Use Mark Areas to Keep and draw across any part of the subject that was wrongly coloured.
  3. Use Mark Areas to Remove for any background that remains.
  4. Click Keep Changes.

The tool works best when the subject contrasts strongly with a plain background.

Adjusting and finishing

Corrections
Brightness, contrast and sharpness.
Color
Saturation, tone and recolouring, for example to greyscale or to one of the theme colours.
Transparency
Fades a picture so that text placed over it stays readable.
Compress Pictures
Reduces the resolution of pictures to shrink the file size, which matters when a presentation must be emailed.
Alt Text
A written description of the picture, read aloud by screen readers. Right-click the picture and choose View Alt Text.
Reset Picture
Discards every adjustment and returns the picture to its original state.
ImportantUse only images you have the right to use: your own, your organisation's, stock images supplied with Microsoft 365, or images whose licence permits it. Credit the source where the licence requires it.

Worked example: a team slide

  1. Insert three staff photographs.
  2. For each, choose Crop > Aspect Ratio > 1:1 and position the face in the centre of the square.
  3. Choose Crop > Crop to Shape > Oval. Each picture becomes a circle.
  4. Set all three to the same height, then use Align Middle and Distribute Horizontally.
  5. Add alt text naming each person.

Key takeaways

  • Resize from the corners; crop rather than stretch.
  • Crop to Shape reshapes the picture's mask to a geometric shape.
  • Remove Background isolates the subject; refine it with the Keep and Remove markers.
  • Compress pictures to control file size and add alt text for accessibility.

Check your understanding

What happens when you use Crop to Shape on a picture?

The picture's mask is reshaped to fit the chosen geometric shape, for example a circle. The image itself is not altered.

A logo looks squashed after resizing. What went wrong and how do you avoid it?

It was resized from a side handle. Reset the picture and resize from a corner handle to keep the proportions.

Remove Background has cut off part of a person's arm. How do you restore it?

Choose Mark Areas to Keep, draw across the missing arm, then click Keep Changes.
