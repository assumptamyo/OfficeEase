Lesson 1.2

Slides that look consistent are not the result of careful dragging. They come from layouts, which fix where each kind of content goes, and from choosing the right view for the task in hand. This lesson covers both.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • explain what layouts and placeholders are and why they matter;
  • select an appropriate layout for a slide and reset a slide that has drifted;
  • choose between Normal, Outline, Slide Sorter, Notes Page and Reading views;
  • describe what Presenter View shows.

Layouts and placeholders

Layout
A predefined arrangement of a slide. It determines which content areas exist and where they sit.
Placeholder
A dotted box on a layout that holds one piece of content: a title, text, a picture, a chart, a table or a video. It carries its own position and formatting.

Content typed into placeholders inherits the design of the presentation. Change the theme later and every placeholder updates; the same is not true of text boxes drawn by hand. Placeholder titles are also what appear in Outline view and what screen readers announce.

The standard layouts

Choose a layout when adding a slide from the arrow under Home > New Slide, or change an existing slide with Home > Layout.

LayoutContainsUse it for
Title SlideA large title and a subtitleThe opening slide
Title and ContentA title and one content areaMost slides: a list, chart, table or picture
Section HeaderA title and a short descriptionAnnouncing a new part of the talk
Two ContentA title and two content areas side by sideText beside a picture or chart
ComparisonTwo content areas, each with its own headingBefore and after, option A against option B
Title OnlyA title and free spaceDiagrams you build from shapes
BlankNothingA full-screen image
Picture with CaptionA picture area and caption textA photograph that needs explaining
TipIf the placeholders on a slide have been dragged out of position or reformatted, click Home > Reset. They return to the position and formatting defined by the layout.

Presentation views

Each view is designed for a different stage of the work. Switch using the View tab or the buttons on the status bar.

ViewShowsBest for
NormalThumbnails, the slide and notesBuilding and editing slides
Outline ViewThe titles and text of all slides as a listPlanning and checking the logic of the argument
Slide SorterAll slides as a grid of thumbnailsReordering a large presentation and judging its overall flow
Notes PageEach slide with its notes as a printable pagePreparing a script or handout
Reading ViewThe show inside the window, with simple controlsReviewing on your own screen without full-screen mode

Presenter View

When a projector or second screen is connected, the audience sees the slide while the presenter's own screen shows Presenter View. It displays:

  • the current slide and a preview of the next slide;
  • the speaker notes for the current slide;
  • a timer and the clock;
  • tools such as a laser pointer, a pen and a zoom.

It is enabled with Slide Show > Use Presenter View. To rehearse with it on a single screen, press Alt + F5.

Common mistakeWriting the full script on the slide because there seems to be nowhere else to put it. Put the script in the notes and read it in Presenter View; keep the slide for what the audience needs to see.

Worked example: an onboarding presentation

  1. Give Slide 1 the Title Slide layout and type the name of the session.
  2. Add a Section Header slide for each part of the session.
  3. Add a Two Content slide with a list on the left and a photograph on the right.
  4. Switch to Slide Sorter view and drag the slides into their final order.
  5. Add notes to each slide, then press Alt + F5 to rehearse in Presenter View.

Key takeaways

  • A layout arranges placeholders; placeholders keep content consistent and theme-aware.
  • Reset restores a slide to its layout.
  • Slide Sorter view shows every slide as a grid and is the best view for reordering.
  • Presenter View shows notes, the next slide and a timer on the presenter's screen only.

Check your understanding

Which view displays all slides as a grid of thumbnails, ideal for reordering a large presentation?

Slide Sorter view.

Which mode shows the presenter their notes, a preview of the next slide and a timer?

Presenter View.

Why is typing into a placeholder better than drawing your own text box?

Placeholder content follows the layout and theme, appears in Outline view and is read correctly by screen readers. A hand-drawn text box does none of these.
