Lesson 4.2

Data becomes information when it is put in order and reduced to what matters. Sorting rearranges the rows; filtering hides the rows you do not need. Neither changes the data itself, and together they answer most everyday business questions.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • sort a list by one column or by several levels;
  • filter by values, by text, number and date conditions;
  • use slicers to filter a table visually;
  • explain the difference between sorting and filtering.

Sorting on one column

Click any cell in the column you want to sort by and choose Data > Sort A to Z or Sort Z to A. The wording adapts to the data: text sorts alphabetically, numbers from Smallest to Largest, and dates from Oldest to Newest.

ImportantSelect a single cell, not a whole column, before sorting. If you select one column and sort it alone, its values are rearranged while the other columns stay still, and every row of your data is silently scrambled. If Excel asks, always choose Expand the selection.

Multi-level sorting

Often one sort is not enough: you want staff grouped by department and, within each department, ordered by salary. This is a multi-level sort.

  1. Click inside the data and choose Data > Sort.
  2. In the first row set Sort by to Department and Order to A to Z.
  3. Click Add Level. Set Then by to Salary and Order to Largest to Smallest.
  4. Click OK. Excel applies the levels from top to bottom.

In the Order list, Custom List sorts by a sequence you define, such as Low, Medium, High, or the months of the year, instead of alphabetically. You can also sort by cell colour or font colour.

Filtering

A filter displays only the rows that meet a condition and hides the rest. Hidden rows are not deleted. Turn the filter arrows on or off with Ctrl + Shift + L or Data > Filter; an Excel Table has them already.

Filter typeExamples of conditions
By valueTick or untick items in the list, such as North and East
Text FiltersBegins with, Contains, Does not equal
Number FiltersGreater than, Between, Top 10, Above Average
Date FiltersThis Month, Last Quarter, Between two dates

You can tell a filter is active because the row numbers turn blue and skip values, and the arrow on the filtered column becomes a funnel. Filters on several columns combine: Region = North and Sales greater than 5,000. To remove them, choose Data > Clear.

TipAn ordinary SUM adds hidden rows too. To total only the visible rows, use the table's Total Row or the function =SUBTOTAL(109, E2:E100).

Slicers

A slicer is a panel of buttons that filters a table. Click in the table and choose Table Design > Insert Slicer, then tick the column, for example Region. Clicking a button filters the table to that value; hold Ctrl to select several. Slicers make the current filter visible at a glance, which suits dashboards and reports used by other people.

Worked example: stock that needs attention

  1. In InventoryTable, open the filter on Quantity and untick 0 to hide products that are out of stock.
  2. Open Data > Sort. Sort by Region (A to Z), then add a level for Sales Amount (Largest to Smallest).
  3. Insert a slicer for Region and click North to see that region alone.
  4. Read the Total Row: it now reports the figures for the visible rows only.

Key takeaways

  • Sorting reorders rows; filtering hides rows. Neither deletes data.
  • Use Data > Sort and Add Level for a multi-level sort.
  • Ctrl + Shift + L toggles the filter arrows.
  • Slicers filter a table with visible buttons.

Check your understanding

Which shortcut turns the AutoFilter arrows on or off?

Ctrl + Shift + L.

You filter a list to show only one department. Have the other rows been deleted?

No. They are hidden and reappear when the filter is cleared.

How do you sort by Region and, within each region, by Sales from largest to smallest?

Open Data > Sort, sort by Region A to Z, click Add Level, then sort by Sales Largest to Smallest.
