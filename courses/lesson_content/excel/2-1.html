Lesson 2.1

Excel treats the number 0.25, the percentage 25% and the text "25%" as three different things. Entering data correctly, and understanding that a number format changes only how a value looks, is the foundation of every reliable spreadsheet.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • enter and edit data efficiently from the keyboard;
  • recognise how Excel classifies text, numbers and dates;
  • apply number formats and explain the difference between Currency and Accounting.

How Excel classifies data

Text
Anything Excel cannot read as a number. Text is aligned to the left and cannot be used in arithmetic.
Number
A value that can be calculated. Numbers are aligned to the right.
Date and time
Stored as a number, with 1 representing 1 January 1900, and displayed as a date. This is why one date can be subtracted from another to count the days between them.
Common mistakeA number that sits on the left of its cell has been stored as text, often because it was typed with a space or an apostrophe. It will be ignored by SUM. Alignment is the quickest diagnostic you have.

Number formats

A number format changes the appearance of a value, never the value itself. A cell containing 0.5 can be shown as 0.5, 50% or $0.50; calculations always use 0.5. Choose a format from the Number group on the Home tab, or press Ctrl + 1 for the full Format Cells dialog box.

Currency versus Accounting

Both add a currency symbol and two decimal places, but they place the symbol differently.

  • Currency puts the symbol directly next to the number.
  • Accounting aligns the symbols at the far left of the cell and the decimal points in a straight column. It shows zero as a dash and negative amounts in brackets.

Accounting is the convention in financial reports because a column of figures becomes easy to scan and compare.

TipIf a cell fills with #####, nothing is wrong with the value. The column is too narrow to display it; double-click the right edge of the column heading to widen it.

Worked example: a price list

  1. Type Item, Price and Discount across row 1 using Tab.
  2. Enter three items with prices such as 1200 and discounts such as 0.1.
  3. Select the prices and apply the Accounting format.
  4. Select the discounts and apply Percentage; 0.1 is displayed as 10%.
  5. Click a discount cell: the Formula Bar still shows the stored value.

Key takeaways

  • Enter moves down, Tab moves right, Ctrl + Enter fills the selection.
  • Text aligns left and numbers align right; dates are numbers in disguise.
  • A number format changes the display, not the stored value.
  • Currency keeps the symbol beside the number; Accounting aligns the symbols at the left edge.

Check your understanding

What is the visible difference between the Currency and Accounting formats?

Currency places the symbol directly next to the number. Accounting aligns the symbols at the far left of the cell and lines up the decimal points.

A cell shows 15% and you multiply it by 200. What is the result, and why?

30. The stored value is 0.15; the percentage format only changes how it is displayed.

A column of figures is aligned to the left and SUM returns 0. What has happened?

The figures are stored as text, not numbers, so they are left out of the calculation.
