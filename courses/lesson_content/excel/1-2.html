Lesson 1.2

A real Excel file is seldom a single grid. Sales data sits on one sheet, charts on another and a summary on a third. This lesson explains how a workbook is organised into worksheets and how to control what you see on screen.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • distinguish a workbook from a worksheet;
  • insert, rename, reorder, colour and delete worksheets;
  • choose a suitable view and keep headings visible with Freeze Panes.

Workbooks and worksheets

Workbook
The Excel file itself, normally saved with the extension .xlsx. One workbook can contain many worksheets.
Worksheet
A single grid inside the workbook, also called a sheet. Each has a sheet tab at the bottom of the window.

Think of the workbook as a ring binder and each worksheet as a page in it. Keeping different kinds of information on separate sheets, such as raw data, calculations and reports, makes a workbook far easier to maintain.

A cell on another sheet is referred to by the sheet name, an exclamation mark and the cell: Data!A1 means cell A1 on the sheet named Data.

TipReplace Sheet1, Sheet2 and Sheet3 with names that describe the contents, such as Data, Charts and Summary. Short names without spaces are easiest to use in formulas.

Workbook views

The view buttons are at the bottom right of the window and on the View tab.

ViewShowsUse it for
NormalThe continuous gridEveryday data entry and analysis
Page LayoutThe sheet as printed pages, with margins, headers and footersPreparing a sheet for printing
Page Break PreviewBlue lines marking where each page endsDragging page breaks so that a table is not split awkwardly

The zoom slider beside the view buttons enlarges or reduces the display. Zoom affects only what you see, not how the sheet prints.

Freeze Panes

In a long list the column headings scroll out of sight, and you can no longer tell what each number means. Freeze Panes locks rows or columns in place while the rest scrolls.

  1. To lock the headings in row 1, choose View > Freeze Panes > Freeze Top Row.
  2. To lock both rows and columns, click the cell just below and to the right of what should stay fixed (for example B2), then choose Freeze Panes.
  3. To release them, choose Unfreeze Panes.

Worked example: setting up a sales workbook

  1. Create a blank workbook and add sheets until there are three.
  2. Rename them Data, Charts and Summary, and give each a different tab colour.
  3. On Data, type headings in row 1 and freeze the top row.
  4. Save the workbook as Sales Tracker.xlsx.

Key takeaways

  • A workbook is the file (.xlsx); a worksheet is one grid inside it.
  • Meaningful sheet names and separate sheets for data, charts and summaries keep a workbook manageable.
  • Normal view is for working, Page Layout and Page Break Preview are for printing.
  • Freeze Panes keeps headings visible while you scroll.

Check your understanding

What is the difference between a workbook and a worksheet?

The workbook is the whole Excel file. A worksheet is one of the sheets, or tabs, inside it.

What does the reference Summary!B4 refer to?

Cell B4 on the worksheet named Summary.

You scroll down a 500-row list and lose sight of the column headings. What should you do?

Use View > Freeze Panes > Freeze Top Row so the heading row stays in place.
