Lesson 2.2

Typing the months of the year, numbering five hundred rows or splitting a column of full names by hand is slow and invites error. Excel can recognise the pattern in what you have typed and complete the rest. This lesson covers AutoFill, custom lists and Flash Fill.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • extend numbers, dates and text series with the fill handle;
  • create a custom list for a sequence your organisation uses;
  • use Flash Fill to split, join and reformat data.

The fill handle

The fill handle is the small green square at the bottom-right corner of the selected cell or range. When the pointer is over it, the pointer becomes a thin black cross; dragging it copies the cell or continues its pattern. This is AutoFill.

To number a list 1, 2, 3, either type 1 and 2 in the first two cells and drag both, or drag a single 1 and then choose Fill Series from the AutoFill Options button that appears.

TipDouble-click the fill handle instead of dragging. Excel fills down as far as the neighbouring column has data, which is ideal for long lists. Ctrl + D fills the cell above into the selected cells.

Custom lists

If you frequently type the same sequence, such as branch names, departments or product lines, teach it to Excel once.

  1. Choose File > Options > Advanced and, under General, click Edit Custom Lists.
  2. Type the entries in order, one per line, or import them from a range of cells.
  3. Click Add, then OK.

From then on, typing any entry from the list and dragging the fill handle produces the rest. A custom list can also be used as a sort order, so that sizes sort as Small, Medium, Large rather than alphabetically.

Flash Fill

Flash Fill learns from an example. You type the result you want for the first row or two; Excel detects the pattern and fills in the remaining rows. Trigger it with Ctrl + E, or from Data > Flash Fill.

ImportantFlash Fill writes fixed values, not formulas. If the source data changes later, the filled results do not update; run Flash Fill again. Always check several rows, because one unusual entry, such as a middle name, can mislead the pattern.

Worked example: building email addresses

  1. Enter first names in column A and surnames in column B, with headings in row 1.
  2. In C2 type the address for the first person, for example john.doe@company.com.
  3. Click C3 and press Ctrl + E. Excel fills an address for every row.
  4. Scan the results for names with unusual spellings and correct any that are wrong.

Key takeaways

  • The fill handle copies a value or continues a series; two cells define the step.
  • Double-clicking the fill handle fills down to the end of the adjacent data.
  • Custom lists teach Excel your own sequences.
  • Flash Fill (Ctrl + E) completes a pattern from an example, but its results are static.

Check your understanding

What is the keyboard shortcut for Flash Fill?

Ctrl + E.

You type 10 in one cell and 20 in the cell below, select both and drag down. What appears?

30, 40, 50 and so on. The two cells tell Excel that the step is 10.

After using Flash Fill you correct a surname in column B. Does the email address update?

No. Flash Fill produces fixed values, so you must run it again or edit the address yourself.
