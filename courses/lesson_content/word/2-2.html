Lesson 2.2

Images explain what words cannot, and headers and footers give a document its identity on every page. Both depend on understanding one idea: how an object is positioned relative to the text.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • insert pictures, shapes and icons and resize them without distortion;
  • choose an appropriate text wrapping option and explain its effect;
  • create headers and footers containing page numbers;
  • vary headers between the first page, odd pages and even pages.

Inserting illustrations

The Illustrations group on the Insert tab provides Pictures (from a file or online), Shapes, Icons, SmartArt diagrams and Charts. When an object is selected, a contextual tab such as Picture Format appears with tools for cropping, styling and arranging it.

To resize a picture, drag a corner handle. Corner handles preserve the proportions; the side handles stretch the image and distort it.

TipAdd Alt Text (right-click the picture) describing what the image shows. Screen readers read it aloud, which makes the document accessible to visually impaired readers.

Text wrapping

Text wrapping determines how the surrounding text flows around an object. Set it with the Layout Options button beside the selected object, or with Picture Format > Wrap Text.

OptionBehaviourTypical use
In Line with TextThe picture behaves like one large character in the line. This is the default.Simple documents where the image sits between paragraphs
SquareText flows around the rectangular frame of the pictureReports and newsletters
TightText follows the actual outline of the imageCut-out images with transparent backgrounds
Top and BottomText stops above the picture and resumes below itWide figures and charts
Behind TextThe picture is placed behind the document text, which runs over itBackgrounds and faint logos
In Front of TextThe picture floats above the text and covers itStamps, signatures and overlays

Every option except In Line with Text makes the picture a floating object that can be dragged freely. A floating object is attached to a paragraph by an anchor; if that paragraph moves to another page, the picture moves with it.

Common mistakeDragging an inline picture and wondering why it will not go where you want. An inline picture can only sit in a line of text. Change the wrapping to Square or Tight first.

Headers and footers

The header is the area in the top margin of each page and the footer the area in the bottom margin. Content placed there, such as a document title, a logo or a page number, repeats on every page.

  1. Double-click in the top or bottom margin, or choose Insert > Header or Footer.
  2. Type the text or insert a picture. The body of the document is dimmed while you work.
  3. For numbering, choose Insert > Page Number and pick a position. Word inserts a field that counts automatically.
  4. Double-click the body text, or press Esc, to return to the document.

Varying headers within a document

The Header & Footer tab contains two check boxes that change the pattern:

Different First Page
Gives the first page its own header and footer, usually left empty so that a cover page carries no page number.
Different Odd & Even Pages
Allows one header for right-hand pages and another for left-hand pages, as in a printed book where the page number sits on the outer edge.

Headers that differ between chapters require section breaks and the Link to Previous setting, which is covered in Module 3.

Worked example: a product sheet

  1. Insert a product photograph and drag a corner handle to reduce it to about a third of the page width.
  2. Set the wrapping to Tight and drag the photograph to the right-hand margin; the description flows around it.
  3. Insert the company logo, set it to Behind Text and lighten it with Picture Format > Color > Washout to act as a background.
  4. Double-click the bottom margin, insert a centred page number, and tick Different First Page.

Key takeaways

  • Resize pictures from the corners to keep their proportions.
  • Wrapping decides how text and image share the page; Behind Text places the image under the text.
  • Floating objects are anchored to a paragraph and travel with it.
  • Header and footer content repeats on every page; the first page and odd/even pages can differ.

Check your understanding

What is the purpose of the Behind Text wrapping option?

It places the image behind the document text, so the text runs over it. It is used for backgrounds and faint logos.

A picture jumps to the next page whenever you add text above it. Why?

It is anchored to a paragraph, and that paragraph has moved to the next page. The picture follows its anchor.

How do you stop the page number appearing on a cover page?

Open the header or footer and tick Different First Page on the Header & Footer tab.
