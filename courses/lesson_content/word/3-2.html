Lesson 3.2

The Layout tab defines the physical page: its size, its margins and how text is divided across it. The central concept of this lesson is the section, the mechanism that lets one document contain pages with different layouts.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • set margins, orientation and paper size, including a gutter for binding;
  • distinguish a page break from a section break and choose the right type;
  • give one page a different orientation from the rest of the document;
  • arrange text in columns and control where a column ends.

Page setup

The Page Setup group holds the three settings that define a page.

SettingOptionsNotes
MarginsNormal, Narrow, Moderate, Wide, Custom MarginsNormal is 1 inch (2.54 cm) on all four sides
OrientationPortrait, LandscapeLandscape suits wide tables and charts
SizeA4, Letter, Legal, Executive and othersA4 is standard in most countries; Letter in North America

A gutter margin is extra space added to the binding edge of the page. If a report will be spiral-bound or stapled on the left, a gutter of about 0.5 inch stops the binding from covering the text. Set it under Margins > Custom Margins.

Page breaks and section breaks

Both are inserted from Layout > Breaks, and they look similar on screen, but they do very different jobs.

Page break
Pushes the following text to the top of the next page. The page settings stay exactly the same. Shortcut: Ctrl + Enter.
Section break
Divides the document into independent sections. Each section can have its own margins, orientation, columns, headers, footers and page numbering.
Section break typeWhere the new section startsTypical use
Next PageOn the following pageA landscape page, or a new chapter with its own header
ContinuousOn the same pageChanging the number of columns part-way down a page
Even Page / Odd PageOn the next even- or odd-numbered pageBooks in which chapters always open on a right-hand page
Common mistakeChanging the orientation and finding that the whole document has turned landscape. Orientation applies to a section, and a document with no section breaks is one single section. Insert the breaks first, then change the orientation.

Different layouts in one document

To turn a single page landscape in the middle of a portrait report, isolate it in a section of its own:

  1. Click at the end of the page before the one that should change and insert Breaks > Next Page.
  2. Click at the end of the page that should be landscape and insert another Next Page break.
  3. Click anywhere in the middle section and choose Orientation > Landscape.

The same principle gives each chapter its own header. Open the header of the new section and turn off Link to Previous on the Header & Footer tab; otherwise the section simply repeats the header of the one before it.

TipTurn on the ¶ marks with Ctrl + Shift + 8 to see where breaks are. Section breaks store the layout of the text above them, so deleting one makes that text take on the layout of the section below.

Columns

Newsletters and brochures use columns because short lines are easier to read. Choose Layout > Columns and pick One, Two, Three, Left or Right. More Columns lets you set exact widths and spacing and add a line between columns.

Text fills the first column before flowing into the next. To end a column early, insert a column break with Ctrl + Shift + Enter.

To keep a full-width headline above a two-column article, place a Continuous section break after the headline and apply two columns only to the section below it.

Worked example: a three-page newsletter

  1. Set the margins to Moderate and the paper size to A4.
  2. After the cover page, insert a Next Page section break; after the chart page, insert another.
  3. Click in the chart section and set the orientation to Landscape.
  4. In the final section, type the headline, insert a Continuous break, then apply two columns with a line between.

Key takeaways

  • A page break starts a new page; a section break starts a new set of layout rules.
  • Orientation, margins, columns, headers and numbering are all properties of a section.
  • Use Next Page breaks to isolate a page, and a Continuous break to change columns on the same page.
  • A gutter margin reserves space for binding.

Check your understanding

What is the difference between pressing Ctrl + Enter and inserting a Next Page section break?

Both move the text to a new page, but only the section break creates a new section whose margins, orientation, columns and headers can differ from the pages before it.

Which break lets you switch from one column to two half-way down a page?

A Continuous section break, because the new section begins on the same page.

A report will be spiral-bound on the left. Which setting protects the text from the binding?

A gutter margin on the left, set in Custom Margins.
