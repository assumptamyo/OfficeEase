Lesson 1.1

Almost every document you will ever produce is shaped on one screen: the Home tab. This lesson explains how Word organises its commands and how to control the appearance of characters and paragraphs deliberately rather than by trial and error.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • identify the parts of the Word window and explain how the Ribbon is organised;
  • distinguish character formatting from paragraph formatting;
  • apply fonts, sizes, emphasis, alignment and spacing using both the Ribbon and the keyboard;
  • remove unwanted formatting cleanly.

How the Word window is organised

Word groups its hundreds of commands in a three-level hierarchy. Understanding the hierarchy means you can find any command by reasoning about where it should be, instead of memorising where it is.

Ribbon
The band of commands across the top of the window. It replaces the old menus and toolbars.
Tab
A page of the Ribbon devoted to one stage of work: Home for editing, Insert for adding objects, Layout for page setup, and so on.
Group
A labelled cluster of related commands inside a tab. The Home tab contains the Clipboard, Font, Paragraph, Styles and Editing groups.
Dialog box launcher
The small arrow in the corner of a group. It opens the full dialog box with every option, including those that do not fit on the Ribbon.

Two further areas are worth knowing. The Quick Access Toolbar above the Ribbon holds commands you want one click away (Save, Undo, Redo). The status bar at the bottom reports the page number and word count and holds the zoom slider.

Two levels of formatting

Word applies formatting at two distinct levels. Confusing them is the most common cause of formatting that "will not behave".

LevelApplies toExamplesGroup
CharacterOnly the text you have selectedFont, size, bold, italic, colour, highlightFont
ParagraphThe whole paragraph containing the cursorAlignment, line spacing, indents, bulletsParagraph

In Word a paragraph is everything up to the next press of Enter, so a one-word heading is a paragraph. Because paragraph formatting belongs to the whole paragraph, you only need to click inside it; you do not need to select it.

TipPress Ctrl + Shift + 8 to show the hidden ¶ marks. Seeing where each paragraph ends makes formatting problems far easier to diagnose.

Character formatting

Select the text first, then apply the format. The essential commands and their shortcuts are:

CommandShortcutUse it for
BoldCtrl + BKey terms and figures the reader must not miss
ItalicCtrl + ITitles of works, foreign words, light emphasis
UnderlineCtrl + URarely; readers mistake underlined text for links
Grow / shrink fontCtrl + ] / [Adjusting size one point at a time
Font dialog boxCtrl + DSmall caps, strikethrough, character spacing

For body text in a professional document, choose one readable font at 11 or 12 points and keep it throughout. Emphasis works only when it is rare.

Paragraph formatting

Alignment

AlignmentShortcutEffect
Align LeftCtrl + LStraight left edge, ragged right. The default for body text.
CenterCtrl + EEach line centred. Used for titles.
Align RightCtrl + RStraight right edge. Used for dates and figures.
JustifyCtrl + JText spread evenly between both margins, as in a book or newspaper.

Spacing and indents

Line spacing controls the distance between lines inside a paragraph; spacing before and after controls the gap between paragraphs. Set them from the Line and Paragraph Spacing button, or precisely in the Paragraph dialog box.

Common mistakePressing Enter twice to separate paragraphs, or pressing the space bar repeatedly to indent. Both break as soon as the text is edited. Use Spacing After and the indent controls instead.

Worked example: formatting a notice

  1. Type the title Staff Training Day and press Enter, then type two sentences of detail.
  2. Click in the title. Press Ctrl + E to centre it; because alignment is paragraph formatting, no selection is needed.
  3. Select the title text, set the size to 18 and press Ctrl + B.
  4. Click in the body paragraph, press Ctrl + J to justify it and set Spacing After to 6 pt.

Removing formatting

When formatting has gone wrong, clearing it is quicker than repairing it. Ctrl + Space removes character formatting from the selection, and Ctrl + Q removes paragraph formatting. The Clear All Formatting button in the Font group does both.

Key takeaways

  • The Ribbon is organised as tabs, then groups, then commands; the dialog box launcher reveals every option.
  • Character formatting needs a selection; paragraph formatting only needs the cursor in the paragraph.
  • Justify aligns text to both margins; control gaps with spacing settings, never with extra Enter presses.

Check your understanding

You click inside a paragraph and press Ctrl + B, but nothing turns bold. Why?

Bold is character formatting, so it applies to selected text. With nothing selected it only affects what you type next.

Which alignment gives a straight edge on both the left and the right?

Justify (Ctrl + J). Word adjusts the spacing between words so every full line reaches both margins.

Where would you look for an option that is not visible in the Font group?

Click the dialog box launcher in the corner of the group, or press Ctrl + D, to open the full Font dialog box.
