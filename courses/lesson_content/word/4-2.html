Lesson 4.2

Academic and professional writing must show where its information comes from and help the reader find their way. The References tab automates both tasks. Its tools generate tables of contents, notes, citations and bibliographies that stay correct as the document changes.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • insert and update an automatic table of contents;
  • add footnotes and endnotes and explain when each is appropriate;
  • record sources, insert citations and generate a bibliography in a chosen style;
  • caption figures and tables and mark entries for an index.

The automatic table of contents

A table of contents (TOC) is built from the heading styles introduced in the previous lesson. Word collects every paragraph formatted as Heading 1, Heading 2 or Heading 3 and lists it with its page number.

  1. Make sure the headings use the built-in heading styles.
  2. Click where the contents should appear, normally on a page of its own after the title page.
  3. Choose References > Table of Contents and select an Automatic Table.

Each entry is a link: hold Ctrl and click to jump to that section.

ImportantThe table does not refresh by itself. After editing the document, click in the table and press F9, or choose Update Table. Select Update page numbers only if only the pagination has changed, or Update entire table if headings were added, removed or reworded.

Footnotes and endnotes

A note attaches a comment or a source to a precise point in the text by means of a small raised number.

NoteShortcutPositionSuitable for
FootnoteAlt + Ctrl + FBottom of the same pageShort clarifications the reader should see immediately
EndnoteAlt + Ctrl + DEnd of the document or sectionLong notes that would crowd the page

Word numbers the notes automatically. If you insert a new note between two existing ones, or delete one, all the others are renumbered.

Citations and bibliography

A citation is the short reference placed in the text, for example (Khan, 2023). The bibliography is the full list of sources at the end. Word stores each source once and formats both for you.

  1. In the Citations & Bibliography group, choose the required Style: APA, MLA, Chicago, Harvard or IEEE.
  2. Click where the citation belongs and choose Insert Citation > Add New Source.
  3. Select the type of source (book, journal article, website) and complete the author, title, year and publisher fields.
  4. To cite the same source again, pick it from the Insert Citation list.
  5. At the end of the document, choose Bibliography and insert a References or Works Cited list.
APA
American Psychological Association. Author and date; common in the social sciences, education and business.
MLA
Modern Language Association. Author and page number; common in the humanities.
Chicago
Often uses footnotes; common in history and publishing.
TipIf your institution changes the required format, simply choose a different Style. Every citation and the bibliography are reformatted instantly; nothing has to be retyped.

Captions, tables of figures and the index

Select a picture or table and choose References > Insert Caption to add a numbered label such as Figure 1 or Table 2. Because the numbers are automatic, they stay in sequence when figures are added or moved. Insert Table of Figures then lists all captions with their page numbers.

An index is the alphabetical list of topics at the back of a long document. Select a term and press Alt + Shift + X to mark it as an entry; when all terms are marked, choose Insert Index.

Worked example: completing a research paper

  1. Set the citation style to APA.
  2. Add three sources with Add New Source and insert a citation after each borrowed idea.
  3. Add a footnote to define a technical term.
  4. On page 2, insert an automatic table of contents.
  5. On the last page, insert the bibliography, then press F9 in the table of contents to include it.

Key takeaways

  • The table of contents is generated from heading styles and must be updated with F9.
  • Footnotes sit at the bottom of the page; endnotes at the end of the document.
  • APA, MLA and Chicago are citation styles; changing the style reformats every citation.
  • Captions and index entries are numbered and collected automatically.

Check your understanding

You add a new chapter but it is missing from the table of contents. What must you do?

Check that its title uses a heading style, then click in the table, press F9 and choose Update entire table.

Name one citation style and say what kind of reference it produces in the text.

APA is a citation style; it produces an author-and-date reference such as (Khan, 2023).

What is the practical difference between a footnote and an endnote?

A footnote appears at the bottom of the page on which it is referenced; an endnote appears at the end of the document or section.
