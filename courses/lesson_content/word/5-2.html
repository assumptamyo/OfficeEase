Lesson 5.2

Important documents are rarely written by one person. Contracts, reports and policies pass between authors, reviewers and approvers. The Review tab makes that process transparent, so that every change is visible, attributable and reversible, and it protects the final version from unwanted alteration.

Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • record edits with Track Changes and choose how they are displayed;
  • accept or reject changes and manage comments;
  • compare two versions of a document;
  • restrict editing and protect a document with a password.

Track Changes

When Track Changes is on, Word records every insertion, deletion, move and formatting change instead of simply applying it. Each change is marked with the reviewer's name and the time, and each reviewer is given a different colour. Turn it on or off with Review > Track Changes or Ctrl + Shift + E.

The Display for Review list controls what you see. It does not change what has been recorded.

Accepting and rejecting changes

The document owner decides which changes stand.

  1. Switch to All Markup and click at the start of the document.
  2. Use Next and Previous in the Changes group to move from one change to the next.
  3. Click Accept to make the change permanent, or Reject to restore the original text.
  4. To finish quickly, open the arrow beneath either button and choose Accept All Changes or Reject All Changes.

Comments

A comment is a note attached to a piece of text; it asks a question or makes a suggestion without altering the document. Select the text and choose Review > New Comment, or press Ctrl + Alt + M.

Colleagues can reply beneath a comment, forming a thread, and mark it resolved once the point has been dealt with. Delete comments individually, or use Delete All Comments in Document before publication.

TipUse a tracked change when you know what the text should say, and a comment when you want the author to decide.

Comparing documents

If someone edited a document without turning on Track Changes, Review > Compare recovers the differences.

Compare
Takes an original and a revised document and produces a third document in which every difference appears as a tracked change. This is sometimes called a legal blackline.
Combine
Merges the tracked changes of several reviewers, each working on their own copy, into a single document.

Worked example: reviewing a supplier contract

  1. Press Ctrl + Shift + E to turn on Track Changes, then correct three clauses.
  2. Select the payment term and add a comment asking the supplier to confirm it.
  3. When the contract returns, step through the changes with Next, accepting or rejecting each one.
  4. Resolve and delete the comments, then check in All Markup that nothing remains.
  5. Apply Restrict Editing with a password so that the agreed text cannot be altered.

Key takeaways

  • Ctrl + Shift + E toggles Track Changes, which records who changed what and when.
  • Markup views change what you see, not what is stored; clear all changes before sending a final copy.
  • Compare reveals the differences between two versions; Combine merges several reviewers' edits.
  • Restrict Editing limits what others may change; Encrypt with Password controls who can open the file.

Check your understanding

You switch to No Markup and email the document. Can the recipient still see what you deleted?

Yes. No Markup only hides the changes. The recipient can choose All Markup and read them. Accept or reject every change first.

A colleague edited your report without tracking. How can you find out what they changed?

Use Review > Compare with your original and their revised file. Word shows every difference as a tracked change.

Why is Mark as Final not a security feature?

It only displays a message and opens the file read-only. Any reader can click Edit Anyway and change the document.
